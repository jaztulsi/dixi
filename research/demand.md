# Demand: where Dixi is needed most

## 0. Summary

1. **The biggest time pools are the work between apps, not inside one app.** Microsoft measures one interruption every 2 minutes. Workers switch apps about 1,200 times a day and spend about 4 hours a week getting their bearings back. They spend 25% of their time hunting for answers. Copying data between apps takes 1.5 to 9+ hours a week. Email, meeting and scheduling tools already sell well (Superhuman, Granola, Calendly). The between-app layer has no owner.
2. **"Where do I click?" is a mass-market problem with no product.** 73% of people 65+ need help with a new device. WalkMe estimates 36 working days a year lost to software frustration (vendor figure). 71% of adults 50+ want tailored tech help. Blind workers ask a coworker for help at least twice a week. Nobody sells a voice that looks at *your* screen and points at the button.
3. **People want guidance and control as well as automation.** In blind users' own words: "Sometimes I just need it to tell me where I am, what options are available, and what I should do next", and "ask me before clicking something important." Only 13% of Americans would let AI help them "a lot", and 61% want more control. Dixi's Talk mode and its yes-gate match what people are asking for.
4. **People stop using assistants because they are slow, unreliable and lack context.** Computer-use agents take a few seconds per step. Copilot's primary-use share fell from 18.8% to 11.5% in six months. Siri's AI upgrade slipped to late 2026 or later. Gemini still can't do some basics that Assistant could. Users keep pasting context into chatbots by hand: 77% of enterprise AI users paste data in.
5. **Willingness to pay is real but narrow.** About 3% of AI users pay (Menlo). ChatGPT has about 50M payers out of about 900M weekly users. Pure voice input supports $12–15/month (Wispr Flow, valued at $2B). Assistive software sells at $94–$187/year for home licenses and $699 for Dragon. Employers' median accommodation cost is $300. Tech support sells as a $180/year bundle (Best Buy).

## 2. What people want from a computer assistant but don't get

### 2a. Evidence of the gap
- **"Tell me" vs "do it".** Blind users in a 3-week diary study (8 users, 1,258 commands, 12 apps) said:
  - "Sometimes I just need it to tell me where I am, what options are available, and what I should do next" (P3).
  - "I want it to ask me before clicking something important" (P7).
  - "If it can explain how the application is organized and guide me through the steps" (P5).

  The best agent (GPT-5) completed only 52.5%. Common failures were ungrounded actions (24.6%), not finding hidden paths (21.4%) and cross-app coordination (7.4%). OneNote (35%) and Excel (44%) were the worst ([arXiv 2609.00524](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00524), fetched).
- **Reddit threads split the same way.** Some people want an agent that "uses the browser/desktop for me instead of just giving advice". Others want only "tell me what to click". Builders describe "copy paste hell" and the Select → Copy → Alt+Tab → paste-into-ChatGPT loop ([Reddit mirror snapshots, 2026](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1t6zrql/snapshots/2026-05-08T22%3A30%3A11.828738Z); mirror site, low confidence).
- **Workers want more control than experts think they need.** 1,500 workers across 104 occupations were positive about automating 46.1% of tasks, mostly routine low-value ones ([Stanford WORKBank, arXiv 2506.06576](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.06576)).
- **The public wants control.** 61% of Americans want more control over how AI is used in their lives. 73% would let AI help with daily tasks at least a little, but only **13% "a lot"** ([Pew 2025-09-17](https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2025/09/17/how-americans-view-ai-and-its-impact-on-people-and-society/)).
- **Delegation targets.**
  - The top tasks workers would hand off are scheduling (36.4%), drafting and sending email (33.5%) and admin (21.1%) ([Workhuman via Training Mag](https://trainingmag.com/?p=25855); undated).
  - 70% would rather delegate notes, filing or scheduling to agents than to people ([Asana Sep 2025 via Workplace Insight](https://workplaceinsight.net/people-are-handing-work-to-ai-agents-even-though-they-are-unreliable-and-lack-accountability)).
- **Usage is still thin.** 55% of US workers rarely or never use chatbots at work, and 29% haven't heard of using them for work ([Pew 2025-02-25](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/02/25/workers-experience-with-ai-chatbots-in-their-jobs/); fielded Oct 2024).

**What follows from this.** The unmet want is an assistant that:
1. already knows what's on my screen (no pasting);
2. answers in about a second;
3. can either point or do;
4. asks before anything that matters;
5. works in every app, not just inside one vendor's.

Siri's planned on-screen awareness is the closest threat, and it is late.

### 4d. Willingness-to-pay takeaways
1. Voice alone earns $12–15/mo when it saves visible minutes every day (Wispr). Dixi's price ceiling for prosumers is about there.
2. Seniors themselves won't pay much (36%); their children and employers will. Disabled workers' tools are paid for by employers or VR, and $300 one-time is the median accommodation.
3. Usage-based AI cost is now normal (Raycast credits, ChatGPT tiers). Agent-mode minutes can be metered; Talk mode should be flat.
