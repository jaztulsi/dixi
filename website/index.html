The problem

If you can’t use your hands, the web barely works.

People with conditions like ALS and cerebral palsy are left with tools that are slow, narrow, or expensive.

1

Voice assistants wait

Siri and Alexa wait until you stop talking, think for seconds, then handle one narrow task, or hand you off somewhere else.

2

AI agents squint at screenshots

Today’s AI browsers screenshot the page and guess pixel coordinates to click. Slow, costly, and easy to break.

3

Assistive hardware is pricey

Eye-gaze systems can cost thousands of dollars, and built-in head tracking is tiring to use for everyday browsing.

How it works

Talk naturally. Runic keeps up.

Your speech streams in live. Every finished part of a sentence is acted on immediately, while you keep talking.

1

Always listening

No button. Start with “Runic” and it’s yours. Follow-ups like “click the second one” just work. TV and conversation are ignored.

2

Acts mid-sentence

“Open YouTube” fires the moment you say it. By the time you finish “…and search for lofi beats”, the results are loading.

3

Reads the real page

Runic reads the page’s actual buttons, links and fields, not pixels, so it can click “Add to cart” on sites nobody pre-programmed.
